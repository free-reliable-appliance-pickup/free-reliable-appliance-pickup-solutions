Why Washer & Dryer Pickup Can Be Different from General Junk Removal

A junk-hauling company primarily values the removal job. Laundry-appliance buyers and repair professionals may value the machine itself. That difference matters. A clean washer that needs a common repair, a dryer that tumbles but does not heat, or a working set being replaced during a remodel may still have reuse or repair value.

That does not mean every broken machine qualifies. Appliances that are heavily stripped, burned, severely rusted, contaminated, missing major components or extremely difficult to access may be declined. Free pickup is based on the complete request and available local demand.

How to Describe a Washer

Tell us whether it powers on, fills, agitates or washes, drains and spins. Mention error codes, leaking, loud bearings, a damaged lid or door lock, water remaining in the tub, a torn front-load gasket or a machine that stops during a cycle. If you do not know what is wrong, choose “unknown” instead of guessing.

Clear photos of the front, controls, inside drum and model/serial label are useful. A complete description can save time and reduce unnecessary calls back and forth.

How to Describe a Dryer

Tell us whether it powers on, tumbles and produces heat. Identify gas or electric if known. Common problems to mention include no heat, long drying times, unusual noise, damaged controls, a door problem or a dryer that runs but shuts off early.

Do not disconnect a gas dryer unless you are qualified to do so. Tell us when a machine is stacked, inside a tight laundry closet, upstairs or behind a narrow doorway.

Homes, Apartments, Landlords & Property Managers

Pickup requests can come from homeowners, renters, landlords, property managers, apartment communities and small businesses. For rental turnovers or multiple units, list every washer and dryer at the property instead of submitting them one at a time. Multiple useful machines at one location can be easier to route efficiently.

For apartments or condos, include building floor, stairs, elevator, loading-zone rules, gate codes and parking restrictions. For houses, tell us whether the machines are in a garage, laundry room, basement, side yard or upstairs.

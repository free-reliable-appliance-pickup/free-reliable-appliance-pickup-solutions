Stockton Stove, Range & Oven Pickup Review

This page is for cooking appliances at Stockton pickup addresses. A working freestanding range is usually simpler to review than a built-in wall oven because built-ins can involve cabinetry and hardwired electrical connections. Tell us exactly what you have before scheduling.

Gas ranges

Report whether the burners ignite and the oven heats normally. Tell us whether the gas connection has already been safely disconnected by a qualified person.

Electric & induction ranges

Report which elements or zones work, whether the oven reaches temperature, and whether the controls and display operate.

Wall & double ovens

Built-in units need extra planning. Include dimensions, cabinet location and whether the appliance is already safely disconnected and ready to move.

Free pickup is qualification-based. Working and reusable units receive priority. A single nonworking cooking appliance generally does not qualify for the standard free route.

What to Send for a Faster Stockton Review

Fuel type

Gas, electric, induction or dual-fuel. If you are unsure, send a clear photo of the appliance and model label.

Tested functions

Tell us which burners or elements work and whether bake, broil, convection, controls and display operate normally.

Access details

Include floor level, stairs, narrow doors, kitchen islands, gates, parking distance and whether the unit is already pulled out.

Condition photos

Show the full appliance, cooktop, oven cavity, controls and any broken glass, missing knobs, dents or other damage.

Gas and Electrical Safety Before Pickup

Do not loosen gas fittings, disconnect hardwired electrical connections or remove a built-in oven unless you are qualified to do so. If a gas range is already disconnected, say so in the request. If you smell gas, stop using the appliance and contact the appropriate utility or qualified professional.

Pickup review is not a gas-service appointment. Safe disconnection and access must be confirmed before the appliance is moved.

Current San Joaquin County Appliance Disposal Alternative

San Joaquin County currently lists ovens and stoves among the appliance categories accepted through County solid-waste facilities, subject to current quantity, separation and facility rules. The County says appliances should be empty and separated from other trash in the load.

Important 2026 change: Foothill Sanitary Landfill stopped accepting all waste on June 28, 2026. San Joaquin County says operations were consolidated at the North County Recycling Center and Sanitary Landfill, with Lovelace Materials Recovery Facility and Transfer Station also remaining available as an alternative facility.

Stockton Stove & Oven Pickup FAQ

Can one working stove or range qualify for free pickup in Stockton?

Yes. A working and reusable stove or range can be submitted by itself. Acceptance still depends on condition, local demand, exact address, safe access and route availability.

Do you review gas and electric ranges?

Yes. Send the fuel type and tested functions. Gas appliances must be safely disconnected before pickup unless another arrangement is explicitly confirmed.

Can a broken stove or oven be picked up free?

A single nonworking stove, range or oven generally does not qualify for the standard free-pickup route. Submit the true condition so the request can be reviewed accurately.

Do you remove built-in wall ovens?

They can be submitted for review, but built-in and hardwired appliances require more preparation. Tell us whether the unit has already been safely disconnected and removed from cabinetry.

Is Foothill Sanitary Landfill still an option?

No. San Joaquin County announced that Foothill stopped accepting all waste on June 28, 2026. Check the County's current facility guidance before self-hauling.

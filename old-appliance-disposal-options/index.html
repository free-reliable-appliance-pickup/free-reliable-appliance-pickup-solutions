How to Dispose of Old Appliances

The best disposal path depends on whether the appliance still works, can be repaired, contains refrigerant, and what collection programs are available locally. Start with reuse when practical, then compare qualification-based pickup, donation or resale, retailer take-back, municipal bulky-item collection, and an approved recycling facility.

  1. Working appliance: consider reuse, resale, donation or a qualifying pickup service.
  2. Repairable appliance: compare repair value with local reuse demand before treating it as waste.
  3. Refrigerator or freezer: use a service or facility that can handle refrigerant-containing equipment correctly.
  4. Nonworking appliance: check the local city, hauler or recycling facility for accepted bulky appliances and any fees or preparation rules.

Do not abandon appliances or place large appliances in regular trash. Local rules vary, so use the city or regional resources below for the actual pickup address.

Start With Condition, Not the Trash Pile

An old appliance is not automatically waste. If a washer fills, drains and spins correctly, a dryer tumbles and heats, a stove or oven operates properly, or a refrigerator maintains safe temperature, the appliance may still have reuse or resale value. Working and repairable appliances can have a very different next step from units that are unsafe, heavily damaged or at the true end of their useful life.

Our own pickup review starts with the same practical question: what condition is the appliance actually in? Photos, model information, a clear description of what works, and honest access details are more useful than simply calling the appliance “old.”

Practical rule: if the appliance still works safely, consider reuse first. If it does not work, compare repair value, local recycling and disposal requirements before deciding what to do with it.

Six Practical Options for an Old Appliance

1. Reuse or Resell

A functioning appliance may still be useful to another household. Clean it, verify the functions you can safely test, photograph the model label and disclose any known defects.

2. Donate

Some charities or reuse organizations accept working appliances, but acceptance rules vary. Confirm the appliance type, age, condition, pickup availability and any required preparation before transporting it.

3. Qualifying Free Pickup

Our service prioritizes fully working major appliances. Mixed loads may qualify when approximately 80% of the appliances work, and minor-repair items may be considered depending on the market and route.

4. Retailer Take-Back

When purchasing a replacement appliance, ask whether the retailer will remove or recycle the old unit. ENERGY STAR specifically identifies retailer take-back as one possible refrigerator/freezer recycling route.

5. Municipal or Utility Program

City, county or utility programs may offer bulky-item collection, appliance recycling, drop-off events or incentives. Eligibility, appointment limits, accepted appliances and fees vary by location.

6. Qualified Recycler

For end-of-life appliances, especially refrigerators and freezers, use an appropriate recycling or disposal provider and confirm how refrigerants and other regulated materials are handled.

Refrigerators and Freezers Need Special Care

Refrigerators and freezers are different from many other household appliances because the cooling system contains refrigerant. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency explains that refrigerant-containing appliances are subject to safe-disposal requirements and that owners should use appropriate collection or recycling channels.

Do not cut refrigerant lines, puncture the sealed system or remove the compressor to prepare a refrigerator or freezer for disposal. EPA guidance specifically tells consumers not to tamper with the appliance before disposal, and federal rules prohibit knowingly releasing most refrigerants during disposal.

EPA suggests options such as a public-works collection program, retailer, rebate or bounty program, or recycler. ENERGY STAR also recommends asking retailers, local utilities, municipal programs or scrap-metal recyclers about refrigerator and freezer recycling and confirming proper handling.

Prepare the Appliance Information Before You Choose an Option

Good information makes every path easier—whether you contact us, a charity, a retailer, a city program or a recycler. Take clear photos of the front, interior when relevant, model/serial label and any visible damage. Note whether the appliance is currently testable and describe known problems without guessing.

For pickup, also record the floor level, number of stairs, doorway and hallway restrictions, elevators, gates, parking limitations and whether appliance doors may need removal for clearance. These details can determine whether a pickup that looks simple on paper is practical in the real location.

California Appliance Disposal, Reuse & Pickup Guide

California's statewide waste strategy increasingly emphasizes reducing waste, reusing and repairing items when practical, and recycling materials that have reached the end of their useful life. CalRecycle classifies major appliances such as washing machines, clothes dryers, water heaters, stoves and refrigerators as major appliances or white goods.

1. Reuse first when the appliance works

A working washer, dryer, refrigerator, freezer, stove or range may still have practical value. Selling, donating or using a qualification-based pickup can keep useful equipment in circulation longer.

2. Check the real city program

California appliance collection rules vary by city and hauler. Los Angeles, Sacramento, Anaheim, San José, Palm Springs and other cities publish different limits, preparation requirements and appointment systems.

3. Refrigerators need the right route

Refrigerators and freezers may have refrigerant-handling or facility rules that differ from washers, dryers or stoves. Do not damage the sealed system to make disposal easier.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best thing to do with a working old appliance?

Start with reuse, resale, donation or a qualifying pickup that can keep the appliance useful. Do not assume a working appliance needs to become scrap.

Does every old appliance qualify for free pickup?

No. Our free pickup is based on appliance condition, load mix, access, location, demand and available coverage.

Can I cut the lines on a refrigerator or freezer before pickup?

No. Do not cut refrigerant lines or remove the compressor as a disposal shortcut. Use an appropriate collection or recycling route.

What if my appliance does not qualify?

Check retailer take-back, utility programs, municipal collection, reuse/donation options or an appropriate local recycler. Verify current local rules directly with the provider.

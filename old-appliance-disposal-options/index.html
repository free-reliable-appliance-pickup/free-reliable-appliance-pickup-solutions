Start With the Appliance Condition

The best disposal option depends first on whether the appliance still works safely. A working refrigerator, washer, dryer, stove or freezer may have reuse value. A nonworking or end-of-life unit may need a retailer, municipal program, utility program, transfer station or recycler.

Working or reusable

Consider reuse, resale, donation or a qualification-based pickup before treating the appliance as waste.

Needs repair

Describe the defect honestly. Some minor-repair items may still have reuse value, but free pickup is not automatic.

End of life

Use an appropriate municipal, retailer, utility or recycling channel, especially for refrigerated appliances.

Six Practical Ways to Get Rid of an Old Appliance

1. Reuse or resale

If the unit works safely, keeping it in use can be the simplest path.

2. Qualification-based free pickup

Our service reviews working and reusable major appliances plus eligible mixed loads. Check the qualification rules.

3. Retailer haul-away

When a new appliance is delivered, ask whether the retailer will remove or recycle the old unit.

4. Municipal bulky-item collection

Many cities or contracted waste haulers offer scheduled appliance or bulky-item collection. Rules and fees vary by address.

5. Utility recycling programs

Some electric utilities or efficiency programs accept qualifying refrigerators or freezers and may offer a rebate or bill credit.

6. Qualified recycling or self-haul

Transfer stations, scrap recyclers and appliance recyclers may accept large appliances. Confirm refrigerant rules before transporting cooling equipment.

When Free Reliable Appliance Pickup May Fit

Free pickup is qualification-based. Fully working major appliances receive priority. Mixed loads may qualify when most major appliances are working. A single nonworking appliance generally does not qualify under the standard free-pickup rules.

Microwaves are accepted only with another qualifying major appliance, and dishwashers require at least two other major working appliances.

Refrigerators and Freezers Need Special Handling

Refrigerators and freezers contain refrigerant and other components that require proper handling. EPA guidance says appliance owners should use appropriate disposal channels and avoid tampering with refrigerant systems before disposal. ENERGY STAR also recommends checking retailer, utility, municipal and local recycling programs.

Do not cut refrigerant lines or remove the compressor yourself. Use a qualified disposal or recycling channel for refrigerant-containing appliances.

Old Appliance Disposal Questions

What is the best thing to do with a working old appliance?

If it works safely, reuse, resale, donation or a qualifying pickup may be better first options than treating it as waste.

Does every old appliance qualify for free pickup?

No. Free pickup depends on condition, access, local demand and route availability.

Can I cut the refrigerant lines on an old refrigerator or freezer?

No. Use an appropriate collection or recycling channel and leave refrigerant handling to qualified professionals.

What if my appliance does not qualify for your pickup?

Check retailer take-back, local utility programs, municipal bulky-item collection or a qualified recycler. Local rules and fees vary.

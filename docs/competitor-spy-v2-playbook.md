# Competitor Spy / V2 SEO Playbook

Use this loop for every priority market before major page expansion.

1. **Search visibility first** — use GSC finalized query/page data plus a current live-search snapshot. Protect pages already near positions 1–10 before rewriting them.
2. **Spy the actual winners** — inspect the top local pages from TakeMyAppliance, AppliancePickupNow and other city-specific competitors for title/H1/meta, city and ZIP depth, appliance-specific guides, request flow, FAQs, schema, commercial intent, public-disposal alternatives and internal links. Adapt useful patterns; never copy text.
3. **Mine customer language** — review local competitor feedback for repeated concerns such as price/fees, confirmation, timing, stairs, gates, inside pickup, photo texting, rescheduling, proof of pickup and professionalism. Add only genuinely missing questions/answers.
4. **Technical pass** — canonical, indexability, sitemap, redirects, metadata/schema synchronization, image quality, broken links and city-vs-appliance cannibalization.
5. **Intent ownership** — general city page owns broad appliance-pickup/removal/recycling intent; dedicated washer/dryer page owns laundry-only intent; regional refrigerator/freezer or other appliance hubs own specialty intent unless a genuinely unique city×appliance page is justified.
6. **Conversion trust** — free to submit where true, no account required where true, direct call/text when a real market number exists, photo-first intake, clear confirmation language, qualification rules and access requirements.
7. **Local usefulness** — city-specific access realities, neighborhoods/ZIPs when useful, official municipal/hauler alternatives, apartments/HOAs/gated properties, local commercial/recurring sources and nearby-city links.
8. **Network truthfulness** — direct/partner-supported markets can use local service language; recruiting markets must say request + partner coverage review and must not imply a permanent local branch.
9. **Internal-link loop** — state/region hub → city → appliance specialty → city/nearby markets → commercial/recurring resources, without spammy repetition.
10. **Recheck** — after deployment, run repository audits, submit changed URLs through IndexNow, then use later GSC data to decide the next improvement instead of rewriting blindly.

Current competitor observations (Oct. 1, 2026):
- TakeMyAppliance: strong city pages, free-to-submit/no-account flow, local details, neighborhoods/ZIPs, commercial section, city×appliance guide depth.
- AppliancePickupNow: photo-first intake, brands/subtypes, reuse-before-disposal, access notes, no-account form, city-specific public alternatives.
- Local junk haulers: direct phone/text, photo quotes, speed/same-day language and strong local operational detail.
- Our differentiators: explicit qualification rules, 80% mixed-load guideline, direct market phone numbers where supported, real photos, access-first review, truthful network coverage, reusable-appliance focus, senior/property-manager/recurring programs and official fallback guidance.

## Competitor alert output contract

Every competitor/ranking alert must be actionable, not just descriptive. Report these fields in order:
1. **Target query + market** — the exact search intent being checked.
2. **Observed competitor move** — what changed or what the current winner is doing.
3. **Competitor strength** — the concrete page/flow advantage, not a vague label.
4. **Our current strength** — what our intended owner page already does better or differently.
5. **Our gap** — the missing signal supported by evidence. If no material gap exists, say **protect** instead of inventing work.
6. **Exact countermove** — the smallest specific page, internal-link, trust, local-detail, conversion or authority change that addresses the gap.
7. **Risk check** — confirm the move will not create cannibalization, a doorway page, false local-presence language, broken canonical/indexing, or a duplicate city page.
8. **Recheck plan** — deploy, run audits, submit changed URLs through the existing free IndexNow workflow, then wait for live-search/Search Console evidence before another rewrite.

The repository audit now mirrors this contract: missing competitor-gap signals emit a `COUNTERMOVE`; 9/9 pages emit `PROTECT`.

## Location-page quality ceiling

The approved indexable location inventory is intentionally capped at **564 pages**. Do not raise that ceiling merely to add another city.

For a genuinely stronger priority market:
- prove the market has distinct local value, route/partner relevance or competitive demand;
- consolidate a lower-priority supporting location back into the appropriate regional hub;
- remove the retired URL from the sitemap and approved-location inventory;
- keep the retired page `noindex, follow` unless a better technical replacement is justified;
- give the freed slot to the stronger city and reconnect its regional/internal-link path.

Dedicated washer/dryer pages belong in the approved service-page inventory only when they have materially distinct laundry intent and useful local content.

## Evidence hierarchy when a tool is unavailable

Use, in order:
1. current live-search evidence for the exact city/query;
2. repository/indexability/internal-link audits;
3. Search Console finalized query/page data when the connected access is available;
4. later crawl/ranking evidence after deployment.

If Search Console access is unavailable, do **not** invent a position or imply that a ranking was verified. Continue with live-search competitor evidence and the free repository checks, then recheck Search Console when access is restored.

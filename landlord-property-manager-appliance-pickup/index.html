Recurring Appliance Pickup Relationships

Property managers often replace appliances throughout the year rather than only once. You can use the same pickup request process whenever a refrigerator, washer, dryer, freezer, stove or range is replaced or left behind.

Turnover Appliances

Submit appliances left after move-out or removed during make-ready work.

Replacement Appliances

If an older unit is being replaced but still works, say exactly what functions were tested. Reusable working appliances receive priority.

Multiple Units

For several apartments or houses, list each appliance and its unit/location separately so the route can be reviewed before scheduling.

Repeat Requests

When another appliance becomes available, submit a new request with current photos, condition and access information. Approval is based on the individual load and current coverage.

Why Working Appliances Matter

Our model is different from ordinary junk hauling. We prioritize appliances that can still be reused, resold, repaired economically or routed to an appropriate local operator. That is why accurate condition information is important.

For landlords, this can create a simple outlet for appliances that are being replaced even though they still have useful life. A refrigerator may be replaced for a remodel, a washer/dryer set may be upgraded, or a range may be removed during a renovation even though the appliance still functions.

Best requests: working washers, dryers, washer/dryer sets, refrigerators, freezers and ranges with clear photos and straightforward access.

Tenant Turnover and Unit Cleanouts

Appliances often need to be evaluated between tenants, after a remodel or when a property owner replaces older equipment. If an appliance still works, say what functions you have verified rather than assuming it is broken because it is being replaced.

For vacant units, confirm whether someone must meet the pickup crew, whether a lockbox or manager access procedure exists, and whether the building has a service entrance or restricted pickup hours. For occupied properties, coordinate access before a pickup time is confirmed.

How to Report Multiple Appliances

For each appliance, include the type, brand when known, approximate age if known, working condition and any visible damage. For example, a request might include one working refrigerator, one working dryer and one washer that does not spin. This is much more useful than saying “three old appliances.”

Mixed loads may qualify when approximately 80% of the major appliances are working. A single nonworking appliance does not automatically qualify for free pickup.

Special rules: microwaves are accepted only with another qualifying major appliance. Dishwashers are considered only when included with two or more major working appliances.

Apartment and Multi-Unit Access

Access can determine whether a pickup can be scheduled. Include the floor level, stairs, elevator availability, narrow hallways, security gates, loading zones, parking restrictions and any tight doorway or turn. If an appliance is in a garage, storage room, basement or outside area, explain the route from the appliance to the pickup vehicle.

If building management requires a specific service entrance, certificate, appointment window or advance notice, mention that before scheduling so the request can be reviewed correctly.

Photos and Condition Documentation

Clear photos help when several appliances are being reviewed. A full front photo of each appliance is the minimum useful starting point. Add model-label photos when practical and show obvious damage. For refrigerators and freezers, include an interior photo and say whether the unit is currently cooling or freezing. For laundry equipment, describe whether the washer fills, drains and spins and whether the dryer tumbles and heats.

A Simple Process for Property Managers

1. Photograph

Take a front photo and, when useful, an interior or model-label photo.

2. Describe Condition

Tell us what works and what does not. Avoid simply labeling an appliance “old.”

3. Give Access Details

Unit number, floor, stairs, elevator, gate, parking, service entrance and pickup window.

4. Submit for Review

We review the load, condition, location, access, local demand and available coverage before confirming pickup.

Preparing Appliances for Removal

Remove tenant belongings, food and personal items and clear the path to the exit. Do not disconnect gas appliances unless the work is handled safely by someone qualified. Tell us whether appliances are already disconnected, but do not create unsafe conditions just to prepare for a request.

For working refrigerators and freezers, keeping them plugged in when practical may help verify condition before pickup. If a unit has already been disconnected, tell us whether it was working before it was unplugged.

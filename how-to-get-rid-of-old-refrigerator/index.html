How to Get Rid of an Old Refrigerator

The best option depends on whether the refrigerator still cools, whether it is complete and safe to move, and what programs serve the pickup address. Start with reuse or qualifying free pickup when the refrigerator still has useful life; use a refrigerant-aware recycling or disposal route when it does not.

The Short Answer

If the refrigerator still cools correctly and is complete, start with a reuse-focused option: qualifying free pickup, resale or donation. If it does not cool, is unsafe, or is truly end-of-life, compare retailer haul-away, a utility or municipal program, or a recycler that can properly handle refrigerant-containing appliances.

Do not cut the refrigerant lines or remove the compressor. Refrigerators require proper handling at end of life.

Five Ways to Get Rid of an Old Refrigerator

1. Qualifying Free Refrigerator Pickup

Working and reusable refrigerators may qualify for free pickup in supported areas. Send cooling condition, photos, exact location and access details. Pickup is confirmed only after review.

2. Sell or Donate a Working Refrigerator

A clean, working unit may still have reuse value. Be accurate about temperature performance, cosmetic condition and known defects.

3. Retailer Haul-Away

When buying a replacement refrigerator, ask whether the retailer offers haul-away. Fees and recycling practices vary.

4. Utility or Municipal Program

Some utilities and local governments operate refrigerator recycling or bulky-item programs. Eligibility is usually tied to the actual service address.

5. Qualified Appliance Recycler

For an end-of-life refrigerator, choose a recycler or facility that can handle refrigerant and other regulated components appropriately.

What Information Helps Before Pickup?

  • Whether the refrigerator is currently cooling.
  • Exterior, interior and model-label photos.
  • Exact city or ZIP code.
  • Floor level, stairs, elevator, narrow doors or gates.
  • Whether the unit is already disconnected and empty.
  • Any missing shelves, doors, handles or visible damage.

Refrigerator Disposal and Refrigerant Safety

Refrigerators and freezers contain refrigerant in a sealed cooling system. The U.S. EPA provides specific disposal guidance for refrigerant-containing appliances. Do not puncture or cut the system to make the appliance easier to remove.

Free vs. Paid Refrigerator Removal

A free route may work when the refrigerator has reuse value and access is practical. Paid removal may be necessary for nonworking units, difficult carry-out, large built-in models, debris-heavy cleanouts or locations outside an active free route. Ask for the full terms before agreeing to service.

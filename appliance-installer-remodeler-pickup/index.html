Appliance Pickup for Installers, Remodelers and Contractors

Installers, kitchen remodelers, builders, contractors and delivery teams can submit old-for-new appliance pickup requests for replacement routes, service exchanges, punch lists and multi-unit projects. A clear request explains the project phase, handoff condition, access path and schedule.

Best starting point: service area or job sites, appliance mix, expected stop count, built-in or oversized units, disconnect status, access rules and the desired pickup cadence.

Common Installer and Remodeler Pickup Work

Old-for-new replacement routes

List refrigerators, ranges, dishwashers, washers and dryers being removed after replacement or installation.

Remodels and project phases

Group appliance removals by job site, phase and target date so pickup can be reviewed as project work.

Service exchanges and exceptions

Separate damaged, incorrect, incomplete, warranty or scrap-intended units from routine reusable take-outs.

Define the Appliance Handoff

Equipment

Type, count, condition, dimensions when oversized, and whether the unit is built-in or freestanding.

Project timing

Give delivery windows, installation dates, expected weekly stops or project milestones.

Access

Include stairs, elevators, parking, narrow doors, loading areas and customer or job-site rules.

Responsibility

State who disconnects utilities, protects floors, stages the old appliance, photographs the handoff and closes the work order.

Safe Pickup and Qualification

Do not assume a pickup provider will disconnect gas, electrical, plumbing or built-in equipment. State the appliance's current connection status and use an authorized professional when the job requires one. Working and reusable appliances are the strongest candidates for free pickup; larger recurring programs are reviewed from the full route, equipment and access picture.

Installer and Remodeler Appliance Pickup Questions

Can installers submit old-for-new appliance routes?

Yes. Residential replacement stops, remodel projects, service exchanges and punch-list appliance removals can be reviewed.

What should installers include for each stop?

Include address, appliance type and condition, built-in or oversized status, access details, utility or disconnect status and the expected handoff time.

Can recurring installer routes be reviewed?

Yes. Daily, weekly or project-based routes can be reviewed when the service area, typical stop count and exception process are defined.

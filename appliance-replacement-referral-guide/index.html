When a customer replaces an appliance: a responsible pickup referral guide

For independent appliance repair shops, replacement-appliance retailers, landlords and property managers who want to help customers explore an appropriate next step for an old machine—without promising service that has not been confirmed.

Use this guide when repair or replacement creates an old-appliance question

A service technician may determine that a repair is not cost-effective; a customer may buy a new refrigerator while the old unit still cools; or a landlord may be replacing several machines. The customer needs a truthful answer about whether an older appliance could be reused, removed or recycled.

Free Reliable Appliance Pickup is a request-and-review network for qualifying major household appliances. Requests are screened for condition, location, access and available local pickup capacity. Submitting a form does not book a pickup. There is no promise that every item or city qualifies.

No affiliation required. Appliance businesses may refer a customer to the public qualification guide without joining the pickup network. A link or editorial mention is optional, and not a condition of any relationship.

Six questions to ask before sending a customer

  1. What item is being replaced? Note the category and quantity: washer, dryer, refrigerator, freezer, range, stove or oven.
  2. Does it actually work? A washer that will not spin or a refrigerator that no longer cools must not be described as fully working. Explain faults honestly.
  3. Is it a single appliance or a mixed load? Working/reusable units receive priority. Minor-repair units may be reviewed. A load that is roughly 80% working may sometimes qualify; one nonworking unit is not automatically eligible.
  4. Where is the appliance? City and ZIP code are needed to check whether a local pickup route is available.
  5. How difficult is removal? State if it is in a driveway, garage, first floor, upstairs or basement; mention stairs, narrow doors and gates.
  6. Can the customer supply photos? Images of the appliance, visible damage and model label can help the team assess it before considering a route.

Important special cases: Microwaves are not accepted by themselves. Dishwashers are reviewed only with at least two other major working appliances. Never promise those items alone qualify.

Wording a repair shop or store can share with a customer

This short note may be adapted for invoices, written repair recommendations or a customer-resource page; describe it as an independent third-party option, not an official partnership unless one exists.

“If you are replacing a major household appliance, you can review the qualification rules at Free Reliable Appliance Pickup and submit its request form. Free pickup is conditional on the appliance's condition, your location and a confirmed local pickup route. An inquiry is not a confirmed appointment. Provide accurate details about whether the appliance works and any stairs or access difficulties.”

For customers with only a broken appliance: If free pickup is not suitable, consider your municipal bulky-item program, a manufacturer's or retailer's delivery haul-away, or a licensed local recycler. Follow appropriate handling requirements for refrigerators and freezers.

What happens after the request?

  1. The customer submits their appliance condition and access details directly through the public request form.
  2. Our team evaluates the item, local reuse demand and available routes. We may request more photos or measurements.
  3. The customer receives an explicit answer or is contacted about the next step. Only a direct confirmation establishes a scheduled pickup.

Businesses referring customers should not collect sensitive customer details unnecessarily or forward private information without permission. Direct the customer to submit their own request.

Transparency for businesses: This page is not a referral commission offer, a guarantee of qualified leads or a promise of exclusive local coverage. Repair shops and retailers should continue to offer their own repair, disposal or haul-away services as appropriate.

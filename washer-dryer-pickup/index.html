What receives priority?

Fully working washers and dryers receive the strongest priority. Matching laundry sets are especially useful because both units can often be reused together. Minor-repair units may also be reviewed depending on model, age, condition, local demand and available coverage.

What should I submit?

Tell us whether the washer powers on, fills, agitates, drains and spins, and whether the dryer powers on, tumbles and heats. Include leaks, noises, missing parts and photos of the front, controls and model label.

Can you pick up from inside?

Inside pickup may be available. Tell us whether the machines are in a garage, laundry room, basement, upstairs area or outside. Include stairs, narrow doors, tight halls, gates and whether the units are disconnected.

Do broken units qualify?

A single nonworking appliance does not automatically qualify for free pickup. Working appliances receive priority. Mixed loads may qualify when approximately 80% of the load is working.

What happens after I submit?

After you submit the appliance details, we review the condition, number of units, access and current local demand. Clear photos and accurate working-condition information help us decide whether the request qualifies. If pickup is available, scheduling depends on the active route and local coverage. Keep the machines accessible and do not move them into a more difficult location before the pickup window.

How to describe a washer or dryer accurately

Washer

If possible, say whether it powers on, fills with water, agitates or washes, drains completely and reaches the final spin. Mention leaks, loud bearing noise, error codes, a locked lid or door, or a damaged control panel. For front-load washers, note any visible door-boot damage or water remaining in the drum.

Dryer

Tell us whether it powers on, tumbles and produces heat. If it is a gas dryer, identify it as gas; if it is electric, say electric when known. Mention unusual noises, a damaged door, missing knobs or controls, or a unit that runs but does not heat.

These details help us review the request faster and reduce the chance of a condition mismatch at pickup.

Sets, Single Machines and Laundry Configurations

State whether you have a complete washer-and-dryer set or only one machine. For a set, test and describe each appliance separately; a working dryer does not make a nonworking washer a fully working set.

Top-load and front-load washers

Identify the loading style and approximate capacity when known. For top-load machines, report lid-lock and agitation problems. For front-load machines, report door-lock, drain, gasket, odor and pedestal details.

Stacked and compact laundry

Clarify whether the units are a single-piece laundry center or two separate machines stacked with a kit. Include overall height, closet clearance and whether the units must be unstacked before removal.

Gas and electric dryers

Photograph the rear connection or model label if the fuel type is uncertain. Gas dryers require qualified disconnection when still connected. Electric dryers may use different outlet and cord configurations, so include the plug in a photo when practical.

Parts and Accessories to Include

Tell us whether washer fill hoses, drain hose, dryer cord, vent, stacking kit or manufacturer pedestals are included. Missing accessories do not automatically disqualify a machine, but complete information helps evaluate reusable condition and plan the pickup.

Do not leave water inside a washer. If it will not drain, disclose that before scheduling because retained water increases weight and can spill during removal.

Preparing laundry appliances for pickup

Remove clothing and personal items, make the path to the exit clear and tell us whether water hoses, drain hoses, gas lines or electrical cords are still connected. Do not disconnect a gas appliance unless you are qualified to do so. If the machines are stacked, in a closet, behind a narrow doorway or on an upper floor, include that information before scheduling.
